Technical guide · Specification

The nameplate is
a laboratory figure.

A module rated at 550 W produces 550 W in a flash test at 1,000 W/m² and 25 °C cell temperature. On a roof at 65 °C it produces roughly 475 W, and the difference is not a defect — it is the assumption a project has to be modelled on.

01Standard conditions

Where the number comes from

One set of conditions
gives one number.

The rating is a measurement protocol, not a prediction.

01

Standard test conditions are fixed and artificial

STC means 1,000 W/m² irradiance, a 25 °C cell temperature and the AM 1.5 reference spectrum. A real installation rarely holds all three at once — which is exactly why every datasheet also publishes the coefficients below.

02

NOCT describes a more realistic cell

Nominal operating cell temperature is measured at 800 W/m², 20 °C ambient and 1 m/s wind, and typically lands between 42 °C and 46 °C. It is the anchor for estimating cell temperature on site, not a second power rating.

03

Every published figure is at STC unless stated

Bifacial gain, low-irradiance behaviour and temperature coefficients are quoted separately because they are corrections to the STC value. Mixing a bifacial figure into an STC comparison inflates one product against another.

02Temperature

The largest single loss

Every degree costs
a fraction of a percent.

On a commercial roof this is the difference between the design and the inverter's nameplate.

01

The coefficient is negative, and it is per degree

Pmax temperature coefficients sit around −0.29 %/°C to −0.35 %/°C for N-type TOPCon and a little higher for PERC. The figure is a percentage of rated power for each degree above 25 °C.

02

Working the example through

At 65 °C cell temperature and −0.34 %/°C, the correction is 40 × 0.34 = 13.6 %. A 550 W module therefore delivers about 475 W — not because it underperforms, but because the rating was measured at 25 °C.

03

Two modules with the same wattage are not equal

A 0.05 %/°C difference between two products is about 2.7 % of output at 65 °C. Over a 25-year model on a large array that gap usually outweighs a small difference in price per watt, so the coefficient belongs in the comparison alongside the rating.

03Degradation

The two-part warranty

Year one, then
every year after.

The financial model needs both figures, and the warranty separates them too.

01

First-year loss is larger than the annual rate

N-type TOPCon modules are typically warranted at around 1 % degradation in the first year, with PERC around 2 %, then roughly 0.4–0.55 % a year thereafter. A model that applies the annual rate from year one will overstate output.

02

Product warranty and power warranty are different documents

The product warranty covers materials and workmanship; the power warranty covers the output curve at a stated year. A lender reads the second, and the year-25 or year-30 figure is the one that has to hold in the cash flow.

03

Ask for the curve, not the headline

A single "84 % at year 25" figure can be reached by different paths. The datasheet's year-by-year table is what the model should use, and it is also the quickest way to compare two products that quote the same endpoint.

04Mismatch

Strings, not modules

Cold is the
dangerous end.

Half-cut cells reduce mismatch loss; string voltage can still exceed the inverter's limit.

01

One weak module affects the whole string

Modules in series carry the same current, so a soiled, shaded or underperforming unit limits every other module in that string. Half-cut cell layouts and bypass diodes reduce the penalty, but they do not remove it.

02

Open-circuit voltage rises as temperature falls

Voc has a negative temperature coefficient of roughly −0.25 %/°C to −0.30 %/°C, which means the highest string voltage of the year occurs on the coldest morning, not at midday in summer. String sizing is checked against the inverter's maximum input at that condition.

03

The inverter window sets the string length

Both ends matter: the cold-morning Voc must stay below the inverter's maximum DC voltage, and the hot-afternoon Vmp must stay above its minimum operating voltage or the array stops producing while the sun is still up.

05Loss stack

From DC to PR

Seven losses,
one ratio.

Performance ratio is the single figure a model is judged on.

01

What sits between the module and the meter

Soiling, mismatch, DC wiring, inverter conversion, AC wiring and transformer, and availability each remove a few percent. Shading is site-specific and can dwarf all of them, so it belongs in the model as a measured figure rather than a default.

02

A well-designed commercial array lands around 0.75–0.85

Performance ratio is actual yield divided by the yield the irradiance alone would allow. Values in that band are normal for a clean, well-ventilated array; a model promising noticeably more is usually carrying an optimistic assumption somewhere.

03

Temperature loss is not in the PR figure

Performance ratio is conventionally calculated against the irradiance-derived energy at the actual cell temperature, so the temperature correction is applied earlier. Counting it twice is one of the most common modelling errors on a project.

Something not covered? Ask our team ↗

Which temperature coefficient should a project assume?+

Use the value on the datasheet of the module being specified, typically between −0.29 %/°C and −0.40 %/°C depending on cell technology. Apply it at the cell temperature the site actually reaches, which is well above ambient on a roof-mounted array.

How does first-year degradation enter the financial model?+

As a separate step before the annual rate. Apply the first-year figure once, then the annual figure for every year after, and check both against the power warranty table rather than a single endpoint figure.

Why does string voltage matter at low temperature?+

Because open-circuit voltage rises as temperature falls. The highest string voltage of the year occurs on the coldest morning, and it is that figure that must stay below the inverter's maximum DC input voltage.

What performance ratio is realistic for a commercial array?+

Roughly 0.75 to 0.85 for a clean, well-ventilated installation. Where a model shows more, check the soiling assumption, the availability figure and whether temperature loss was counted twice.
